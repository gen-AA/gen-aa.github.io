# Data &amp; Evaluation

## Data

- **LLM-generated books (Subtask A):** over 200 long-form books (50K+ words each) in the English dataset, generated by ten frontier LLMs and covering 12 genres sourced from Project Gutenberg. The same generation pipeline is used for every LLM, and human-authored Gutenberg books serve as few-shot references, so eras and genres mirror the human data. The data has been extended to multiple languages, including Italian, Spanish, German, Russian and Chinese (over 1K books across six languages overall), and may be scaled to additional frontier LLMs. Each book is monolingual.
- **Human-authored books (Subtask B):** over 500 books from Project Gutenberg by a balanced pool of 50 authors with varying levels of prolificity, to reduce stylistic bias and ensure diversity.
- **Unified data (Subtask C):** the human-authored and LLM-generated books combined, enabling attribution across both kinds of authors and cross-source generalisation analysis.

### Quality and cleaning

LLM-generated books are assessed with fluency, diversity and repetition metrics (perplexity, self-BLEU, self-repetition, n-gram diversity and compression ratio), and a human evaluation on a subset of books. Books are cleaned of encoding errors, formatting inconsistencies and LLM generation markers, and remaining boilerplate is removed through manual inspection and n-gram analysis. Beginning and ending sections that may contain identifying metadata (author, title, year) are trimmed to prevent shortcuts. Genre splits are made independently for each author, so a genre that is OOD for one author may be in-distribution for another.

The dataset will be released through a permanent DOI on Zenodo, with gating during the challenge to prevent data leakage.

## Metrics

- **Macro-averaged F1** for attribution performance, giving equal weight to every author.
- **AUROC and AUPR** (threshold-free) for the OOD-Author setting, distinguishing texts from seen versus unseen authors.
- **Final score:** the harmonic mean across all OOD settings within each subtask.

We will also analyse the relationship between Subtask C performance and the average of Subtasks A and B, to see whether unified attribution is a combination of the isolated settings or is harder than either.

## Baselines and tools

Participants will receive an evaluation script and baselines for development, including traditional n-gram models and recent methods such as [TRACE](https://github.com/anudeex/TRACE).
